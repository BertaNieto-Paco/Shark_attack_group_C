# Shark_attack_group_C
Business case:
The company organizes ocean recreation tours (surfing, scuba diving, and shark-watching) and provides data-backed safety ratings and scheduling guidelines for travelers. In this case, considering the company already sells marine tourism packages and wants to create a more data-informed product strategy.
We can compare recorded incident frequency, severity, fatality, location, activity, season, and time patterns.

Main hypothesis: 
Shark incident frequency and severity vary significantly according to location, activity and season and have decreased over the years.


Key business questions:
• Which destinations are the safest for water sports?
(Which coastal regions have the lowest incident rates relative to high tourism activity?)
• Where should we offer guided shark-watching tours?
(Which locations have reliable, non-fatal encounter histories suitable for controlled marine tourism?)
• Which activities are associated with the highest number of recorded incidents, and how severe are those incidents?
(How do surface activities like surfing compare with submerged activities like scuba diving in recorded incident frequency and fatality percentage? 
• When should tours and lessons be scheduled to minimize risk?
(Are there specific times of day or seasons when incidents spike, such as crepuscular dawn/dusk feeding hours?)
• Is ocean recreation actually getting safer over time?
(Have overall attack counts and fatality percentages decreased over the past decade?) 


H1 — Destination: Recorded shark incidents and fatality percentages differ across countries and coastal regions.
Data to use: fatality and incident percentage per country
H2 — Activity: Surface activities such as surfing have a higher recorded incident frequency but a lower fatality percentage than submerged activities such as scuba diving. 
Data to use: fatality and incident percentage per activity
H3 — Shark-watching destinations: Some locations show repeated shark encounters but relatively low historical fatality percentages, making them candidates for further investigation as controlled shark-watching destinations. 
Data to use: fatality and incident percentage per region + species
H4 — Risk Reduction: Global unprovoked shark attacks have decreased over the past decade, significantly reducing the incident risk for ocean tourists.
Data to use: year, fatality, type
H5 — Seasonality: Shark incidents show seasonal patterns, with some months or seasons having higher recorded incident frequencies. 
Data to use: seasons and incidents relation

Visualisation outcomes:
Geographic heatmap of incidents in the past 50 years
Map of areas that are safer for certain sport types
Diagramme /comparison with decreasing attack numbers or fatality percentage  
Places for shark watching
Safety tips for most dangerous/fatal sport type
*For fun: comparisons of chances to die by a shark or a coconut
